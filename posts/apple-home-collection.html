Apple Home Collection makes accessories feel first-party

Cameras, locks, and thermostats co-designed with LG and Schneider Electric promise one-tap setup and no extra accounts.

By Iian KehnOctober 13, 20263 min read

Apple is entering the smart-accessory market without putting its own logo on every camera, lock, and thermostat.

Apple Home Collection is a co-designed range built with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric. The lineup includes four cameras, a dual-camera video doorbell, a smart deadbolt, two thermostats, and temperature and humidity sensors. Products begin arriving in December in the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada, with some accessories following in early 2027.

The unifying feature is not the manufacturer. Each device begins setup with a tap on iPhone, appears directly in the Home app, works with Siri, and requires neither a separate app nor a second account. Thread is supported across the lineup, and network credentials update automatically when the household changes Wi-Fi.

Cameras without another subscription

LG’s Video Doorbell uses a 2.5K front camera and a second full-HD camera aimed at packages. The Floodlight Cam adds motion-activated lighting and color night vision, while the Outdoor Cam offers a simpler angle-adjustable design. The Indoor Cam can physically tilt down when household members return, providing a visible sign that monitoring has stopped.

With a compatible Apple home hub, the cameras can recognize people, familiar faces, pets, vehicles, and packages. An eligible iCloud+ plan stores ten days of encrypted event history and can add natural-language search and automatic clip descriptions. Local storage is designed to keep recordings going during a Wi-Fi outage.

Apple says the video is processed on the home hub or through Private Cloud Compute. Home app data and camera footage are end-to-end encrypted so Apple, LG, and Schneider Electric cannot read them.

Access and climate join the same system

LG’s Smart Deadbolt Lock uses Ultra Wideband to detect an approaching person carrying a compatible iPhone or Apple Watch and unlock automatically. A tap, PIN pad, or hidden physical key provides alternatives. It can also report whether a door is open, closed, or being tampered with. Matter and Aliro support keep core access functions local, including when the internet is unavailable.

LG and Schneider Electric each provide a smart thermostat. Apple’s Adaptive Temperature feature can adjust heating and cooling around presence, bedtime, and time away without a manual schedule. In supported parts of the United States, Clean Energy Guidance can shift demand toward cleaner or less expensive periods. Standalone room sensors and sensors already inside HomeView and HomePod feed the same system.

The most interesting part of Apple Home Collection is its attempt to remove the seams that make smart homes exhausting. A single scene can lower a camera, change the temperature, and start music; leaving can lock the door and begin monitoring. The hardware comes from partners, but setup, identity, control, automation, and privacy are meant to feel like one Apple product.
