Apple’s next launch may put touch on the MacBook Pro

A reported October 28 media experience is expected to bring an OLED touch-screen MacBook Pro, a refreshed iPad mini, and more M6 Macs.

By Iian KehnOctober 15, 20262 min read

Apple’s October hardware calendar may have a second chapter, and this one could redraw the line between the Mac and iPad.

Apple is reportedly inviting selected media to an all-day product experience in Los Angeles on October 28. Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman says the session will center on touch-screen MacBooks, a new iPad mini, and M6 Macs.

The format sounds closer to Apple’s recent HomeView briefings than a traditional keynote. That still makes the invitation meaningful: a hands-on media event gives Apple space to explain unfamiliar hardware in detail, especially if the MacBook Pro is about to gain touch input for the first time.

A display change with larger consequences

The expected MacBook Pro pairs an OLED panel with direct touch control. Either change would be substantial on its own. Together, they raise questions about the physical design of the notebook, how macOS adapts to fingers, and how Apple keeps the Mac distinct from an iPad with a keyboard.

The report points to multiple M6 Macs, most plausibly the 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro. An M6 iMac is another possibility after two years without an update, although any desktop announced late in October may not ship until November.

The iPad mini is also expected to move to OLED. Separate reporting has pointed to a slightly larger 8.4-inch panel, but a 60Hz refresh rate would keep ProMotion reserved for more expensive iPads. A newly listed Smart Folio with a different model number has added another clue that the next mini may change physically.

One launch, two computing directions

A touch-screen MacBook and an OLED iPad mini would arrive from opposite ends of Apple’s computer lineup. One adds a tablet-like input method to the company’s most capable notebook; the other modernizes Apple’s smallest tablet without turning it into a Pro device.

Apple has not announced the event or the products publicly, so the date and lineup remain reports rather than promises. If the briefing happens as described, the important story will not be M6 alone. It will be how Apple explains touch on a Mac after years of insisting that notebooks and tablets deserve different interfaces.

Original reporting

This report is independently written from William Gallagher’s reporting at AppleInsider, which cites Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman on the October 28 media experience. Apple has not publicly confirmed the event. Featured illustration created for CORE.
