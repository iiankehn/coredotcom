Apple opens self-repair parts for iPhone 18 Pro and its newest Macs

Displays, batteries, cameras, and logic boards for the iPhone 18 Pro join parts for the latest Mac Studio and Mac mini.

By Iian KehnOctober 20, 20262 min read

Owners who want to fix an iPhone 18 Pro themselves can now buy genuine Apple parts for it, along with the manuals and tools to do the job.

Apple has added the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max to its Self Service Repair Store, along with the Mac Studio with M5 Max or M5 Ultra and the latest Mac mini, which Apple lists as the M6 or M5 Pro model. Repair manuals for all of them are now on Apple's Self Service Repair site.

What's available

For the iPhone 18 Pro models, the parts list covers most of the phone: display, battery, back glass, enclosure, main and front cameras, logic board, top and bottom speakers, main microphone, SIM tray, and Taptic Engine. Parts can be bought with the tools a repair needs, such as suction cups, adhesive cutters, nylon probes, torque drivers, and replacement screws, or bought separately.

The Mac Studio list ranges from Thunderbolt and USB-C ports to fans, speakers, SSD modules, power supplies, and logic boards. The Mac mini list is shorter but includes fans, speakers, logic boards, batteries, and power supplies.

9to5Mac reports that the rear camera for iPhone 18 Pro costs $249, the same as for last year's model, and that returning the original part earns a $59.76 credit, bringing the effective price to $189.24. Prices for other parts weren't detailed.

Parts while the phone is still new

Apple launched Self Service Repair in 2022, and it has since become a routine stop for each new generation. Adding this year's Pro models now makes official parts available while the phones are still current, rather than once they are old enough to need a battery.

The program still isn't the simplest repair path. The manuals are detailed and the tools are specialized. For many owners, an Apple Store or authorized repair provider remains the easier choice. But for people who already repair their own devices, or independent shops that work outside Apple's network, having genuine parts and documentation from the start matters.

Including desktop Macs also shows the program has grown beyond phones and laptops. Studio and mini owners can now replace fans, storage modules, and power supplies with Apple-supplied parts, instead of relying on secondhand components or the original warranty.
