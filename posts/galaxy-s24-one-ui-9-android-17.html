Android 17 reaches Samsung’s Galaxy S24 and sixth-generation foldables

One UI 9 is rolling out in the United States with new Galaxy AI tools, privacy alerts, scam detection, and automatic video reframing.

By Iian KehnOctober 12, 20262 min read

Samsung’s newest software is moving beyond this year’s phones and onto some of its most capable 2024 hardware.

The Android 17 rollout has reached the Galaxy S24, S24 Plus, S24 Ultra, Galaxy Z Fold 6, and Galaxy Z Flip 6 in the United States. The update arrives as Samsung’s One UI 9 interface and is also appearing in South Korea, with more regions expected to follow.

Owners can check for it through the standard software-update page in Settings. As with any staged release, availability may vary by region, carrier, and device even after the rollout begins.

More than a version number

One UI 9 adds My FanCam, which can automatically track and reframe a subject during video capture. Now Brief gains cards that can be generated and edited, while warranty and care information moves into Settings where it is easier to find.

Samsung is also extending its Galaxy AI suite with Now Nudge, Creative Studio, Interpreter, Document Scan, and Voice Recorder features. Security Brief, Privacy Alerts, and Scam Detection give the release a welcome defensive side alongside the more visible AI additions.

The Galaxy S24 family and sixth-generation foldables remain high-end devices, so their place near the front of the queue is unsurprising. Still, the update is an important measure of Samsung’s long-term support promise: current software is reaching phones that are no longer the company’s newest showcase.

The wider rollout is still forming

The Galaxy S26 line began receiving One UI 9 earlier, after the software debuted on Samsung’s latest foldables. The Galaxy S25 series and Galaxy Z Fold 5 had not joined this specific rollout at the time of the report, though they are expected to follow.

For S24 and Fold or Flip 6 owners, the practical advice is simple: back up important data, leave enough free storage for a large system update, and let the staged rollout arrive rather than relying on unofficial packages.
