Fitbit Edge puts a screen back on the fitness tracker

Google’s $179.99 wearable combines a wide AMOLED display, improved GPS, deep health sensing, and a replaceable battery without becoming a full smartwatch.

By Iian KehnOctober 12, 20263 min read

Fitbit Edge occupies the middle ground between a discreet activity band and Google’s more demanding Pixel Watch.

Google has announced the Fitbit Edge, a $179.99 fitness tracker built around a wide pill-shaped AMOLED display. The screen is protected by Gorilla Glass 3 and offers 46 percent more display area than 2023’s Charge 6.

The 11.5mm aluminum body carries a physical side button for reliable control during workouts and swimming. It is rated IP68 and 5ATM for dust and water resistance, while an altimeter adds elevation data and redesigned hardware promises GPS accuracy 40 percent better than the Charge 6.

Health hardware without the smartwatch overhead

The sensor array includes heart-rate monitoring, ECG, blood-oxygen sensing, skin temperature, motion sensors, and ambient-light detection. More than 50 workout types can provide real-time guidance, and upgraded automatic activity detection is designed to learn habits and recognize more than 100 activities over time.

The interface borrows from the Pixel Watch, with customizable faces and swipeable tiles for exercise, sleep, heart data, cycle health, weather, timers, alarms, and music controls. Google Maps directions are included, but the lack of NFC means Google Wallet is not.

Edge works with Android and iOS, though quick message replies remain limited to Android. It can share an account with Fitbit Air and Pixel Watch 5, allowing data to follow a user who switches between devices.

A repairable battery with a clear tradeoff

Battery life is rated at up to seven days when the always-on display is disabled, falling to roughly two days when it stays active. Five minutes on the magnetic USB-C charger provides about a day of use, and a complete charge takes around 90 minutes.

More unusually, the device can be opened for battery replacement. That gives the Edge a better chance of remaining useful after its original cell wears down—an important advantage for a health product meant to collect years of trends.

Preorders are open now in four finishes, with general availability scheduled for October 27. A Stephen Curry edition with a custom band costs $219.99.
