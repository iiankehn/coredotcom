Logitech gives Googlebook’s mouse and keyboard controls a desktop upgrade

Logi Options+ has arrived for Google’s Android-based laptops with peripheral customization, firmware updates, and support for Magic Pointer.

By Iian KehnOctober 8, 20262 min read

Googlebook is beginning to gain the software that turns a laptop platform into a real desktop ecosystem.

Logitech has released Logi Options+ for Googlebook, bringing its familiar mouse and keyboard control software to Google’s Android-based computers. The app was previously centered on Windows and macOS, where it manages everything from extra buttons to device firmware.

On Googlebook, Options+ can personalize mouse buttons and keyboard keys, adjust pointing and scrolling behavior, and deliver firmware updates for supported accessories. Those controls matter on a platform trying to stretch Android beyond touch-first apps and into full workstations.

A bridge to Magic Pointer

The app also enables Magic Pointer on compatible Logitech mice. Googlebook’s feature connects pointer actions to Gemini and Circle to Search, giving the mouse a role beyond traditional clicking and scrolling.

That makes Logitech’s arrival more than a compatibility checkbox. A customizable mouse can turn AI shortcuts and frequent commands into physical buttons, helping Googlebook behave more like a purpose-built desktop instead of a phone interface enlarged for a laptop screen.

Support is not complete at launch. Logitech describes the current feature set as limited, though the compatibility list already includes prominent products such as the MX Master 4, MX Keys S, Lift, and Pebble Keys 2.

The ecosystem test begins

Googlebook launched with early gaps around web apps, Linux software, and some phone integrations. Options+ addresses a different but equally practical concern: whether everyday desktop accessories can expose their full capabilities instead of falling back to basic Bluetooth behavior.

The app is available now through Google Play. Its arrival gives buyers a better path to a familiar desk setup—and shows that established accessory makers are starting to treat Googlebook as its own computing platform.
