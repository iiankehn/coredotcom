No Man’s Sky is squeezing its universe into iPhone and iPad

Hello Games is bringing the full-scale space adventure to Apple’s mobile devices on October 28, complete with touch, controller, mouse, and keyboard options.

By Iian KehnOctober 12, 20262 min read

A game built around an effectively endless universe is about to become something players can carry in a pocket.

Hello Games will release No Man’s Sky for iPhone and iPad on October 28. This is not a separate miniature edition: the studio says the mobile release contains the same universe and recent content available on other platforms, including the COSMOS update and the customizable large starships introduced with Voyagers.

The game’s core loop remains intact. Players begin near the edge of a procedurally generated galaxy, then explore planets, gather resources, build bases, upgrade ships, and work toward the center.

A mobile interface, not a smaller ambition

Hello Games has built a new touch-control scheme for the smaller screens. On iPad, players can instead use a controller, mouse, and keyboard, or cast the game to a television for a larger display.

The initial mobile release follows some of the Nintendo Switch version’s compromises. Multiplayer and settlements will not be available on day one. Everything else is being compressed into an installation smaller than 5GB—an unexpectedly modest footprint for a game defined by scale.

The port is the result of years of platform work. Hello Games brought No Man’s Sky to Mac in 2023 after adopting Apple’s Metal graphics technology, including MetalFX tools for upscaling and improving frame rates. That foundation helped open the route to iOS and iPadOS.

Low-friction entry

Preorders opened October 12 as part of Apple’s App Store Indie Games Week. The release will be free to try, with a $19.99 purchase unlocking the full game.

The larger story is what the port says about mobile hardware. A decade-old desktop and console game arriving largely intact on phones and tablets makes the boundary between “mobile game” and “full game” harder to defend—especially when an iPad can shift from touch controls to a desk setup without changing devices.
