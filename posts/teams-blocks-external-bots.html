Teams gains a gatekeeper for meeting bots

Administrators can now stop identified external bots in the meeting lobby before they become an unnoticed attendee.

By Iian KehnSeptember 25, 20262 min read

Meeting bots have become familiar enough to blend into the background. Microsoft’s newest Teams control is designed to make unfamiliar ones harder to admit by mistake.

Teams administrators can now automatically block identified external bots through meeting policies. When the protection is enabled, external bots appear distinctly in the meeting lobby, giving organizers a clearer opportunity to review them before anything joins the conversation.

The feature matters because recording and transcription bots are already routine in online meetings. A malicious or unexpected bot could look like another ordinary notification, particularly when participants are moving quickly through a crowded lobby.

A delayed security control arrives

Microsoft originally planned the feature for May 2026. It ultimately shipped in a Teams update released September 23.

The same update also unifies app and agent installation management. Administrators can work from either the Microsoft 365 admin center or the Teams admin center, and installation changes synchronize across Teams, Outlook, and Microsoft 365 Copilot.

The new blocking option does not eliminate the need to check who is waiting, but it gives organizations a more deliberate boundary between useful automation and an uninvited observer.
