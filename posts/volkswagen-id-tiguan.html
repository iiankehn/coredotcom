Volkswagen turns the Tiguan into its next mass-market EV

The ID.Tiguan will replace the retired ID.4 with familiar branding, conventional controls, and a United States release planned for later.

By Iian KehnSeptember 28, 20262 min read

Volkswagen is giving one of its best-known names an electric future.

The company has confirmed that the upcoming ID.Tiguan will replace the discontinued ID.4 crossover, shifting its EV strategy toward a badge that drivers already recognize.

The ID.Tiguan is scheduled to arrive in Europe in early 2027 alongside Volkswagen’s expanding ID.Polo and ID.Cross lineup. A United States release is also planned, although Volkswagen has not announced when the vehicle will reach the market here. Its final design remains hidden beneath camouflage.

A familiar name for a reset

The change closes the chapter on an EV that helped establish Volkswagen in the mass-market electric segment. The ID.4 recorded roughly 950,000 sales beginning in 2021, but production at the company’s Chattanooga, Tennessee, factory ended in April 2026.

Volkswagen appears ready to carry forward the ID.4’s practical strengths while correcting some of its most criticized design decisions. The ID.Tiguan is expected to restore physical controls for volume, climate settings, and all four windows.

It will also use an Android Automotive-based system, though Volkswagen has not confirmed whether the vehicle will adopt the new Rivian-developed electrical architecture planned for future models.

The result is less a retreat from electric vehicles than a reset: familiar branding, more conventional controls, and another attempt to make an electric crossover feel like a natural replacement for a gasoline-powered family vehicle.
