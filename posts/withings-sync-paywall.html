Withings turns health-data syncing into a $100 subscription

New accounts must pay for Withings+ before watches and scales can automatically export data to Apple Health, Health Connect, and other apps.

By Iian KehnOctober 15, 20263 min read

A Withings device can still measure your health without a subscription. Moving those measurements somewhere else is the part that now costs extra.

Withings has changed how new customers can export data from its watches, scales, and other health devices. Anyone who creates an account on or after October 13 must have an active Withings+ subscription to keep automatic transfers running to third-party services such as Apple Health and Google’s Health Connect.

The membership costs $9.95 per month or $99.50 per year. Withings includes a one-month trial with its hardware, so the restriction may not become obvious until after a new device has already been set up. If the trial ends without a paid plan, syncing pauses; information already transferred to another app is not removed.

Existing accounts keep the old access

The cutoff applies to accounts, not hardware. Withings says people whose accounts existed on or before October 12 will retain free third-party syncing, even if they later add a new watch or scale. New accounts face the subscription requirement regardless of which compatible Withings device they register.

That distinction avoids taking a long-standing feature away from current users, but it creates two ownership experiences for the same hardware. One customer can move measurements into a broader health record at no additional cost, while another buying the same product must maintain an annual plan.

Withings frames the change around new privacy measures and its AI Connector, a system intended to let outside AI services work with a user’s health information. The company’s own support pages, however, explicitly extend the subscription requirement to ordinary exports into Apple Health and Health Connect.

Interoperability becomes a recurring fee

Health platforms are most useful when measurements from different devices can meet in one place. A scale, blood-pressure monitor, sleep tracker, and phone may all come from different companies, while Apple Health or Health Connect acts as the shared record between them.

Putting that connection behind Withings+ changes the value of the hardware. The charge is not only for coaching, analysis, or another layer of service; it is also the toll for automatically moving a customer’s own readings into other software.

Grandfathering existing accounts makes the transition quieter, but the lasting precedent is larger. A device can be fully functional on its own and still become less useful inside the ecosystem a buyer already has. For new Withings customers, data portability is now part of the subscription decision.
